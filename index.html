Hello! I am a researcher in experimental atomic physics at the Agency for Defense Development (ADD), South Korea's national defense R&D institute. At ADD, I have been working on atom-based quantum sensing as part of my mandatory military service. I built experimental systems to develop optically pumped magnetometers and demonstrated their use in the field.

Drawing on my experience with collective control and optical readout of atomic ensembles, I hope to extend my work on atom-light interactions to programmable atomic systems with single-atom control and tunable interactions. I am particularly interested in exploring quantum many-body phenomena in optical lattices and using optical tweezer arrays for quantum computing and precision measurement.

I have applied to PhD programs for Fall 2027 entry. Feel free to get in touch if you'd like to discuss any of these research topics.

Aging test of an atomic vapor cell with Al2O3 wall coating on cubic glass (DOI: 10.1364/AO.572036)

This paper reports a high-temperature long-term aging test of Al₂O₃-coated cubic rubidium vapor cells, demonstrating a nearly fourfold extension in stable rubidium number density and a 4.6-times slower decay compared to uncoated cells.

At APS DAMOP 2025, I presented a poster titled “Lifetime extension in rubidium vapor cells by Al2O3 coating” and co-authored a separate poster titled “Demonstration of atom spin gyroscope operating with high bandwidth over 100 Hz”, led by Dr. Sin Hyuk Yim.

The Research Officer for National Defense (ROND) program selects 20 research officers nationwide for STEM-based national defense research, through which mandatory military service is fulfilled by conducting full-time research at the Agency for Defense Development (similar in structure to Israel’s Talpiot program).
